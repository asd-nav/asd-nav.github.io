Training Generalist Navigation Policies via

TL;DR: A VLM teaches itself to navigate from language by explaining recorded demonstrations — no human goal or reasoning labels needed.

Highlights

  1. Self-distillation from trajectory-only data. One open-source VLM serves as both teacher and student. During training, the teacher sees the demonstrated trajectory and infers the navigation goal and a reasoning trace. The student learns to produce the same reasoning and trajectory from the camera image and the goal. No human-written goals or reasoning traces are used.
  2. Steerable navigation with structured reasoning. In the same scene, the policy reaches different targets when given different directional, color, or object commands. Each action comes with a short trace covering perception, prediction, and planning. Training uses about 100 hours of navigation data, compared with 9,500 hours for OmniVLA.
  3. Zero-shot real-world generalization. We evaluate on 13 real-world tasks with a FrodoBot rover. Although the training data is mostly outdoor, the policy handles unseen indoor scenes and multi-stage instructions. It improves success by 30 percentage points over OmniVLA indoors and over NaVILA on multi-stage tasks, and reaches 95% success outdoors.
  4. Outperforms SFT on frontier-model labels. Fine-tuning on reasoning labels from Claude Opus 5 reaches 36% success on our ablation tasks; self-distillation reaches 80%. On the Left chair task, ASD finishes in 15 seconds, while prompting Claude Opus 4.7 to drive takes over 14 minutes.
